## What it says

Karpathy reports a rapid shift in his own coding workflow from roughly 80% manual/20% agents in November to 80% agent/20% edits in December, describing programming increasingly as 'programming in English.' He cautions that the 'no IDE' and 'agent swarm' hype is premature, noting that agents still make subtle conceptual errors, make wrong assumptions without checking, overcomplicate code, and need close supervision in a large IDE. He argues the main gain is not just speedup but expansion of what can be built, and that leverage comes from giving agents declarative success criteria (tests first, browser MCP, naive-then-optimize) rather than imperative instructions. He also flags risks: manual-coding atrophy, a 'slopacolypse' of AI-generated content in 2026, and open questions about the 10X engineer, generalists vs specialists, and society's bottleneck on digital knowledge work.

## Claims drawn from this source

- **Workflow shifted from manual to agent-driven coding.** Karpathy went from ~80% manual+autocomplete and 20% agents in November to ~80% agent coding and 20% edits+touchups in December, programming mostly in English.
- **No-IDE and agent-swarm hype is premature.** He advises watching agents like a hawk in a large IDE on the side, since models still make mistakes.
- **Agent errors are subtle conceptual mistakes, not syntax.** Models make wrong assumptions and run along without checking, don't seek clarification or surface tradeoffs, are sycophantic, overcomplicate code, and leave dead code.
- **Plan mode helps but needs lightweight inline form.** Things get better in plan mode, but there is some need for a lightweight inline plan mode.
- **Agents are relentless and increase stamina bottleneck.** Agents never get tired or demoralized and keep trying for long stretches, dramatically increasing stamina for work.
- **Gains are expansion more than speedup.** He does more than he would have before—coding things not previously worth coding and approaching code blocked by knowledge/skill gaps.
- **Leverage comes from declarative success criteria.** Give agents goals and success criteria rather than instructions: write tests first, use a browser MCP, write a naive correct algorithm then optimize it.
- **Manual coding ability is atrophying.** Generation and discrimination are different brain capabilities; he can still review code even as writing it manually declines.
