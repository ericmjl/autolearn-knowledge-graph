## What it says

ChainCheck is an evaluation-validity audit for evidence-sufficiency benchmarks. The benchmark question is whether a model can tell a complete passage set from one whose reasoning chain is broken, but every standard way of making a negative leaves a trace: deletion lowers query relevance, bridge substitution puts an unexpected entity in a fluent passage. The ordinary score, a pair AUC of positive over negative, mixes the response to the broken chain with the response to the construction trace. ChainCheck pairs validator-certified counterfactual cells with artifact-only baselines and reports, next to the nominal score, a chain effect, an edit effect and chain selectivity as paired conditional contrasts. Its headline finding is that near-perfect nominal scores do not establish multi-hop reasoning: in an exploratory crossed control on 2,214 HotpotQA pairs, a language-model scorer that never reads the chain reaches a nominal AUC of 0.997 and a one-string match reaches the maximum of the crossed chain-sensitivity statistic CS4 (0.500), while the 11 evaluated systems score 0.308 to 0.484. Trained readouts are edit-dominated on MuSiQue (edit effects 0.312 to 0.441, single-layer probe chain effect -0.025), while on 2WikiMultiHopQA the same MuSiQue-trained readouts show large chain effects (+0.303 to +0.466) and 5 of 6 positive selectivity. On an independent HotpotQA set of 2,276 pairs, the only set with one pair per document group and informative clustered intervals, neither pre-specified pattern replicates: 5 of 6 trained systems are edit-dominated and the reference judge's selectivity lower bound is -0.007. Judge scores also depend on batch layout, and records share documents, so earlier nominal rankings are not general confirmation.

## Claims drawn from this source

- **Near-perfect nominal sufficiency scores conceal construction artifacts.** In the exploratory crossed control on 2,214 HotpotQA pairs, a per-passage language-model scorer that never reads the chain reaches a nominal AUC of 0.997, and a string-match reference (AB3) that checks whether the bridge string in the earlier passage equals the later-hop title reaches the CS4 maximum of 0.500, while the 11 evaluated systems score CS4 from 0.308 to 0.484.
- **ChainCheck reports chain and edit sensitivity as separate paired conditional contrasts.** The protocol admits a pair only when a deterministic validator (checks Q1 to Q9, including byte-identical untouched passages, identical passage positions and Q8 lexical neutrality) certifies every counterfactual cell. It reports the chain effect CE = AUC(B>D) - 0.5, the edit effect EE = AUC(A>B) - 0.5, and chain selectivity Sigma = CE - |EE|, each a paired conditional contrast over cells whose labels follow mechanically from validated interventions.
- **A broken chain without any edit cannot be constructed.** Cells are indexed by chain status and edit trace; the broken chain under a shared later-passage edit is D (bridge entity X swapped for Y in the later-hop supporting passage only), and the intact edited cell is B (X swapped for Y in both earlier and later passages). C, a broken chain without an edit trace, cannot be built without an intervention, so the contrasts are conditional, not an additive decomposition.
- **Trained readouts are edit-dominated on MuSiQue.** On 324 real-entity test pairs, every system trained on A versus D treats the intact but edited chain B almost as a negative: edit effects lie between +0.312 and +0.441. The single-layer probe shows no chain sensitivity at all (chain effect -0.025 [-0.080, 0.031]); structured readouts and adaptation raise the chain effect but the edit effect stays larger, so every trained system has negative selectivity. The zero-shot judge is the only chain-selective system (+0.210 [0.133, 0.287]) with the lowest nominal score (0.889).
- **Edit dominance does not replicate on 2WikiMultiHopQA.** The H1 pattern (edit effect lower bound above 0.25 and selectivity upper bound below zero) fails on 295 real-entity pairs: the MuSiQue-trained readouts keep large edit effects but also show large chain effects between +0.303 and +0.466, and 5 of the 6 systems covered by H1 have positive point estimates of selectivity. Edit sensitivity replicates; edit dominance does not.
- **An independent HotpotQA set shows neither pre-specified pattern replicating.** On 2,276 pairs from the HotpotQA training split (one pair per document group, K_eff = 2,276, the only set allowing a decisive clustered verdict), the H1-type pattern holds for 5 of the 6 trained systems (M1 is not edit-dominated) and the H2-type pattern fails for the reference judge (selectivity lower bound -0.007). The MuSiQue and 2Wiki passes of H2 therefore do not transfer to an independent sample.
- **Judge scores depend on batch layout.** Records are sorted by length and packed into batches of at most 8,192 tokens. Adding new records changed old record batching: on the 8,856 affected records, 82.7% of scores are bit-identical, the A>D outcome flips in 17 of 2,214 pairs (0.77%), all with score gaps below 10^-3, and one record moves by 0.992. Gemma-4-31B-it failed the reproducibility gate (minimum Spearman 0.990383), so scores are reproducible under fixed layouts, not invariant to batching.
- **Document dependence limits informative intervals.** Records share documents: the MuSiQue confirmation records form 52 document groups (220 records, K_eff = 18.22, largest-group rule) and 2Wiki records form 30 groups (436 records, largest group 76.1%). Neither dataset yields an informative clustered interval; 2Wiki has fewer than two effective groups in every slice, so rankings and verdicts rest on point estimates, not general confirmation.
