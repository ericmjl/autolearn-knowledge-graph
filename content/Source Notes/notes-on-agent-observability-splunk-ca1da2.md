## What it says

Splunk argues that reliable agents need more than one check: its Agent Observability platform evaluates behavior, observes performance, tracks token cost, and enforces guardrails across 100% of production traffic on one platform. The central economic claim is that purpose-built Eval-G1 small language models score and protect every interaction at a fraction of the cost of a frontier judge, so full-coverage evaluation is economically viable instead of a sampled slice. Tracing every step from request to response, with latency and errors read next to quality and security signals, turns root-cause analysis from days to minutes and blocks or steers risky actions in real time.

## Claims drawn from this source

- **Reliable agents need more than one check.** Splunk evaluates behavior, observes performance, tracks cost, and guards every agent on one platform, across 100% of your traffic.
- **Purpose-built Eval-G1 models score 100% of traffic at a fraction of frontier-judge cost.** Evaluations and guardrails run on purpose-built small language models called Eval-G1 instead of frontier models, which makes it economically viable to score and protect 100% of production traffic rather than a small sample.
- **Guardrails run on every interaction, not a sampled slice.** Evaluation and guardrails run on every interaction in production, not a sampled slice.
- **Tracing pinpoints where quality, performance, or behavior broke down.** Trace and map agent workflows from request to response, then read latency and errors next to quality and security signals like hallucinations, bias, prompt injection, and PII leakage, so a failure points to its cause instead of a symptom.
- **Root-cause analysis shrinks from days to minutes.** It shortens root-cause analysis from days to minutes, catches quality and safety issues like hallucinations and PII leakage before they reach customers, and enforces guardrails in real time rather than flagging problems after the fact.
- **General-purpose monitoring cannot see an agent's reasoning, tool calls, or output quality.** General-purpose monitoring does not see an agent's reasoning, tool calls, output quality, and more; Splunk Agent Observability instead brings together evaluation, observability, tokenomics, and guardrails so teams can prove agents are right and know what they cost.
