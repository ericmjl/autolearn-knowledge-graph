## What it says

The source is a Wikipedia disambiguation page for 'oversight'. Across its meanings, it treats oversight as checks and controls over a body: in state governance it is the concept of separate branches or agencies exercising authority over one another, ending in Congressional oversight of U.S. federal agencies and other institutions; it also names internal oversight bodies (the UN Office of Internal Oversight Services, fair governance), registration, inspection, standard setting, and enforcement over external parties (auditors via the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, IFAC via the Public Interest Oversight Board), and a French Thoroughbred racehorse.

## Claims drawn from this source

- **Oversight spans governance, audit, and sports.** The page maps 'oversight' across governance (regulation, checks and balances, Congressional oversight, internal oversight), institutional accountability (auditors, IFAC), and sports (a French Thoroughbred racehorse).
- **In governance, oversight is checks and balances.** Oversight in state governance is the concept of separate branches of government or agencies exercising authority over one another.
- **Congress oversees federal agencies.** Congressional oversight over U.S. federal agencies and other institutions is exercised by the United States Congress.
- **Internal oversight bodies audit the organization from inside.** The UN internal oversight is handled by the Office of Internal Oversight Services.
- **External parties face registration, inspection, standard setting and enforcement.** Auditors are overseen through registration, inspection, standard setting and enforcement (Public Company Accounting Oversight Board); IFAC is overseen by the Public Interest Oversight Board.
