Agent synthesis

Overuse of coding agents degrades debugging ability within months and compounds organizational risks such as trust affliction, skills erosion, and social detachment. The mechanism is a feedback loop: agents let humans delegate implementation and review generated output, but effective supervision requires the very coding and debugging skills that overuse erodes, so the more agents are used, the harder supervision becomes. Lars Faye's "Agentic Coding is a Trap" argues that skills can atrophy and dissipate within months, citing an Anthropic study reporting a 47% drop-off in debugging skills, and that this paradox undermines agent use even among experienced engineers; it also notes that replacing direct code work with review truncates juniors' learning, since review is at best half of the learning process. The Wikipedia entry "Software agent" adds the organizational and cultural layer: agents can raise work contentment and self-controlled work, but also carry trust affliction, skills erosion, privacy attrition, and social detachment. No single source says the whole story alone: Lars Faye documents the rapid technical skill loss and the supervision paradox, while "Software agent" frames the broader organizational and cultural costs. Together they show that agent overuse is not merely a personal skill problem but a compounding organizational risk, where the same delegation that speeds output weakens the human judgment needed to supervise it and reshapes trust, skills, and social connection.
